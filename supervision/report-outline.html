One approach to create a report

My colleague Aisha Umair kindly shared the outline below, and I have made some modifications.

The Report Outline

  • Front page
    Should at least include:
    • Project type
    • Project title
    • Student name(s) and SDU email(s)
    • Month and year
    • Supervisor names
  • Abstract
    Should outline the project by addressing the topic, motivation, aim, methodology, results, and conclusion.
  • Acknowledgements (if any)
  • Contents
  • List of Figures
  • List of Tables
  • List of Listings (pseudocode, algorithms…)
  • Acronyms (if any)
  • Glossary
    An alphabetical list of project-specific terms along with their definitions helps readers grasp unfamiliar or specialized terminology used in the report.
  • Introduction
    In this section, you could write about:
    • Context
    • Motivation
    • Problem
    • Scope
    • Objectives
    • Expected contributions
  • Background/Related Work
    This section should examine similar work on the same topic found in the literature. It may also include a segment on relevant concepts that assist the reader with your project.
  • Existing System (if any)
    In this section, you should present the current system if your project is based on it or an extension of it. Topics to include are:
    • Architecture
    • Implementation
  • Proposed System
    Here you could add:
    • Requirements (functional and non-functional)
    • Requirement prioritization (MoSCoW)
    • Design diagrams (UML, sequence diagrams, flow diagrams)
  • Implementation
    Here you could add:
    • How you implemented your system.
  • Testing/Experiments and Results
    Consider all possible test cases and scenarios, and create a separate section for each to evaluate the efficacy of your proposed methodology.
  • Evaluation/Discussion
    Here you could discuss the
    • shortcomings of your project,
    • how they could be mitigated, and
    • your reflections on the results.
  • Conclusion and Future Work
    Here you could
    • summarize your work, and
    • write how the work could be extended in the future.
  • References
    IEEE format (one approach: IEEE Referencing Style Sheet.pdf).
  • Appendix (if any)
    • A, B, etc.

Code Snippets

Consider carefully why you are including code snippets in your report. Classical software engineering diagrams often provide much more value.

Number of pages

☝️ The quality of your content and the concise delivery of your message always outweigh the number of pages in your report.
  • BSc projects in a two-student group:
    • Typically 40 "normal pages"
    • 15 fewer "normal pages" for a one-student group, 20 more for a three-student group
  • BEng projects in a two-student group:
    • Typically 50-60 "normal pages"
    • 20 fewer "normal pages" for a one-student group, 25 more for a three-student group

A "normal page" is defined as 2400 characters, including spaces and excluding figures, tables, and so on. Reference: SI-Generelt-Form og Aflevering.pdf
